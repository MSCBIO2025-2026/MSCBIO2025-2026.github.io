# Course summary
Computers have become essential tools for biology, as well as other scientific disciplines. This course will provide experience using the Python programming language to solve problems and analyze biological data. We will also cover general principles of computational analysis that will help students become more productive researchers. Topic areas include:  
- Introduction to the Python language  
- Data handling and visualization  
- Analysis of different types of biological data, including genetic sequences, protein structures, and images  
- Best practices for designing and critically evaluating computational analyses  

We will use [Python 3](https://www.python.org/download/releases/3.0/) to introduce methods and assignments, along with [Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/) notebooks. The syllabus, course web page, and additional resources will all be hosted on [GitHub](https://github.com/).  


# Grading and assessment
Credit will be assigned as follows:  
- quizzes (30%)  
- in-class work (40%)  
- projects (30%)  

Short quizzes intended to assess and reinforce understanding will be given on paper at the beginning of class. No outside materials (notes, computers, etc.) can be referred to during a quiz unless otherwise specified. The three lowest quiz grades for the semester will be dropped when computing final grades.  

Additional in-class work will appear in the form of worksheets or interactions with teams of students. These components of the course are graded based on completeness/participation rather than correctness.  

One major component of the course will involve long-term projects with teams of students. These projects will introduce new topics in computational biology and work stepwise through large-scale analyses, building to a short presentation at the end of the project. Assessment will be based on the group's ability to perform, understand, and explain the steps of their analysis.  

### Policy on AI use
We will not use AI in the classroom, instead thinking about code on paper, on the board, or in short snippets. Students are free to complete work outside the classroom however they like, with or without AI tools.  

However, grades in this course are based on the ability to **understand**, **justify**, and **explain** the details of a computational analysis in person and without external resources. It is each student's responsibility to ensure that they use AI responsibly to meet these goals.
